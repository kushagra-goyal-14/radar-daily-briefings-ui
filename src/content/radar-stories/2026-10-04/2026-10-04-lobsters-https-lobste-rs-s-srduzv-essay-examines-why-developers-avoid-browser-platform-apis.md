In an opinion essay, Nolan Lawson examines why developers do not consistently “use the platform” when browser APIs could replace custom JavaScript or third-party libraries. The discussion remains an essay rather than a documented ecosystem change.

Lawson attributes platform skepticism to historical browser gaps, ecosystem familiarity, uneven documentation, developer enjoyment, and limited understanding of platform behavior. Examples include modal dialogs, CSS patterns, and ClickHouse storage, where custom designs may duplicate built-in capabilities.

The essay argues that AI coding could either improve platform-idiomatic implementation choices or increase duplicated and over-engineered code. These possibilities are not systematically measured; the author says personal AI-coding experience has shown both patterns.
