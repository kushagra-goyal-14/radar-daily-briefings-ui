Michal Wallace describes a programming pedagogy centered on small, understandable systems. His proposed progression starts with the B4 Forth-like virtual machine, then moves through Lisp or Forth, a Pascal-like language, and implish, with tests structured as interactive lessons.

Wallace says B4 combines bytecode, registers, stack operations, memory access, structured-programming macros, and extensible host-language operations. He also describes using several AI systems to help formalize Lean mathematics during the Jacobian Challenge.

The interview presents these as personal projects and viewpoints, not established industry results. Wallace is now focused on Minavo, plugin-based authoring tools, and “auto-informalization” intended to make formal proofs easier for humans to understand.
