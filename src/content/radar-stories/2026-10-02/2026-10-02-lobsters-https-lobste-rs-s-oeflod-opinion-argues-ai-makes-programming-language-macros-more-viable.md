An opinion article argues that AI-assisted programming changes the balance of risks around customizable language constructs. It presents Sweetener, a TypeScript-oriented macro system, as a vehicle for experimenting with language design.

The examples show custom operators, algebraic data types with pattern matching, and control-flow extensions in TSX. The author argues that macros can encode recurring conventions and help AI-generated code apply them consistently across a codebase.

The thesis is relevant to language and tooling engineers considering extensibility, but the supplied evidence is speculative. It includes no systematic implementation details, performance measurements, adoption data, or independent evaluation of Sweetener.
