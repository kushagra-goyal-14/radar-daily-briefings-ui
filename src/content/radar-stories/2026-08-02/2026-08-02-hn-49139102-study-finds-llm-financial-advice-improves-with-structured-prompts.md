An MIT Sloan study examined long-term financial outcomes from advice generated by large language models. Researchers simulated people aged 22 to 89 following recommendations from GPT-5.2, GPT-5.6, and Gemini 3 Flash.

The models generally promoted saving, diversified stock funds, and lower stock exposure later in life. However, they handled unemployment poorly and often allowed portfolios to drift without active rebalancing. More detailed prompts containing financial and economic assumptions improved results.

The study also reports wealth differences linked to gender, financial literacy, and prior AI experience. Because the results are simulated and demographic benchmarks remain unclear, engineers should treat prompt design and bias evaluation as central requirements for financial AI systems.
