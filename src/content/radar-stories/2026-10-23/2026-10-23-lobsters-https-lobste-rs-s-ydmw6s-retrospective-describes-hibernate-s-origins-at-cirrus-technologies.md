Charles Miller’s retrospective recounts Hibernate’s origins at Cirrus Technologies around 2001. The account connects the project to a troubled Java application involving Enterprise JavaBeans and a debate over whether to retain entity beans or use hand-written SQL.

Miller says Gavin King and Daniel Bradby proposed a persistence framework influenced by Smalltalk frameworks. After management rejected that approach for the immediate project, King began writing its first classes; Hibernate was later released under the Lesser GNU Public License with the cirrus.inf package name.

The article offers architectural and organizational context for Hibernate’s beginnings. Its details are firsthand recollections from a peripheral participant and lack technical internals or independent corroboration.
