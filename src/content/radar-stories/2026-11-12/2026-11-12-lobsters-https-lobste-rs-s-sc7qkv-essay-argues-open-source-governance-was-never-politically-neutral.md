A foss.life opinion essay argues that open source was never politically neutral. It presents the free-software movement as rooted in questions of freedom, autonomy, ethics, and social solidarity, and describes the 1998 “Open Source” label as a pragmatic rebrand.

The essay contrasts corporate governance, including GitHub’s enforcement of U.S. export controls, with community rules such as Codeberg’s reported restrictions on predominantly AI-generated repositories. It argues that moderation decisions determine who bears abuse, spam, and infrastructure costs.

For maintainers, the piece offers a framework for discussing platform rules and labor boundaries. Its claims are argumentative and unsystematically sourced, so it should not be treated as an operational guide.
