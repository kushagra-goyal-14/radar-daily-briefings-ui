INTERPOL’s African Cyberthreat Assessment Report 2026 says artificial intelligence was involved in 55% of recorded cybercrime cases across Africa. The assessment covers data from 36 countries and identifies online scams as the most common cybercrime form in 2025.

According to INTERPOL, criminals are using AI with social media and mobile-money services, including for convincing business-email scams, deepfake-enabled sextortion, and synthetic identities. Reported cybercrime-related financial losses rose from $192 million in 2024 to $484 million.

The assessment also highlights scam centres, regional differences, and weak information sharing among banks, telecom companies, and law enforcement. The supplied evidence does not include the report’s methodology or independently validate its figures.
