Graham’s essay examines five reasons people may dislike AI coding: treating software as art, viewing automation as employment theft, experiencing prompt-driven work as dehumanizing, defining engineering through formal rules, and opposing major AI companies.

The article contrasts typing code with delivering valuable software and argues that generated code can be judged by outcomes rather than authorship. It also frames prompt-mediated development through historical accounts of mechanized intellectual labor.
