A historical account traces BSD Unix from Berkeley’s first software distribution in early 1977 through 2BSD and 3BSD. Early BSD bundled the Pascal system and ex for installation on an existing Unix system; 2BSD later added vi, the C shell, and other tools.

At Berkeley, Ozalp Babaoglu’s virtual-memory work was integrated into VAX 32/V with help from Bill Joy. Joy and others ported BSD tools, while the Pascal system was also adapted, producing 3BSD, which began shipping in December 1979.

The account connects these developments with Unix portability and research-software distribution. It is historical context, not evidence of a current release, compatibility guarantee, or actionable implementation plan.
