Green Mountain Power operates a Vermont virtual power plant built from more than 5,500 home battery systems and other distributed resources. The program provides about 110MW of power and supports households during outages.

Software coordinates participating batteries, rooftop solar, electric-vehicle batteries, commercial demand response, and utility-scale storage. Customers retain reserve power, while the utility draws on aggregated resources when needed to manage peak demand.

The case study describes reduced reliance on conventional peaking plants and reported customer savings, but those figures come from cited company or institutional sources. Enrollment costs, utility incentives, and wholesale-market integration remain significant barriers.
