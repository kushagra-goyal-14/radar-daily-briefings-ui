Ploum’s essay argues that open-source projects should make an explicit choice about accepting LLM-generated contributions. It concludes that projects without a firm position should reject such contributions for now.

The essay compares acceptance, compromise, and rejection policies. It discusses possible effects on contributor participation and community cohesion, while raising concerns about dependence on proprietary tools, reduced human understanding of code, future maintenance, and incompatible copied licenses.

This is an opinion-based governance framework, not an empirical evaluation or implementation report. Its long-term risks are presented as possibilities, so maintainers would need project-specific evidence before adopting the recommendation.
